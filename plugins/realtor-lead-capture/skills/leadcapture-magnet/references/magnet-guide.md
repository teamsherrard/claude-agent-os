# Lead Magnet Guide — what to build and why it works

A lead magnet earns the email. It works when it solves **one real problem** for **a clearly defined reader** —
one reader for a niche guide, or the whole audience under one brand-level promise for a brand-led guide —
fast, and leaves them thinking "if the free thing is this good, I want this agent." Build from the offer.

## The FIRST magnet is always the relocation guide (house rules #11)
Campaign one is locked: a **relocation guide** — *"Moving to [City]? Start Here."* Relocation is the #1
highest-intent search traffic in every market; someone planning a move is the most valuable lead there is,
and they're searching for exactly this before they ever talk to an agent. Locking it removes the overthinking
that stalls agents. (Small market → title it on the metro people search — house rules #11.) The shapes
further down are for the **second campaign onward.**

### Relocation guide structure (built from the 5-question intake + the Brain)
Same skeleton as every guide — **THE PROMISE → the pages → HOW [AGENT] HELPS NEXT** (the output standard's
three bands). The two bookends aren't numbered pages; the **body is 7 pages**, relocation-shaped:

**The promise (the skeleton's THE PROMISE band)** — *"Moving to [City]? Start Here"* + who it's for: anyone
moving to [City] from [the Q1 places].

1. **The framing page** — moving to a city you don't know is the highest-stakes purchase there is; here's the
   way through. Work in **the #1 question they always get** (intake Q3) — it's the reader's question too.
2. **Coming from [Place]? What changes** — the comparison page (intake Q1), one short block per feeder place:
   what your money gets you there vs. here, commute, cost of living, weather, the big practical differences.
   **Numbers, strictly sourced (house rules #5):** the [City] side from `identity/market.md` (their price
   bands) and, if a dated block exists, `memory/market-data.md` (benchmark price, days on market — quote the
   named source + month); the feeder side and anything like tax or cost-of-living ONLY from a quick sourced
   check noted beside the figure (source + month). **No number you can't source — write that point
   qualitatively instead** (*"noticeably more home for the money than [Place]"*). Never from memory.
3. **Where to live — the communities that fit** (intake Q2) — the 3–5 communities the agent chose, one short
   block each in the SAME shape so a reader 2,000 km away can shortlist: price band (from `market.md`) ·
   housing types · what it's genuinely near and how it sits relative to the city's main work hubs (commute) ·
   schools by name · **the honest trade-off** (the one thing a local would warn you about — the commute, the
   construction, the wind) · the day-to-day feel of the **place** (walkable, quiet, lock-and-leave).
   **Describe the place, never the people** — no "suits / ideal for / perfect for [families, retirees, young
   professionals]", never who "belongs" (Fair Housing — house rules #5).
4. **What everyone gets wrong** (intake Q5) — the honest, experience-based page competing guides won't write.
   Misconceptions, surprises, the unflattering-but-true detail. This is the page that earns trust.
5. **The mistakes relocating buyers make** — timelines, buying sight-unseen, wrong-community regret,
   underestimating costs, trusting a listing photo over a drive-by. Experience-based, specific.
6. **What to do, and when** — the relocation order of operations, stage by stage: 6–12 months out (shortlist
   2–3 of the communities above, talk to a local lender about financing across provinces/states, visit once
   if you can) · 3–6 months (get pre-approved, set the real budget, line up the sale back home) · 1–3 months
   (virtual or in-person tours, the offer, the inspection) · moving month (possession, utilities, the first-week
   list). Keep it to what's true in [City] — no invented timelines or rules.
7. **What good looks like** — the relocation story from intake Q4 (real only; if none, a short "here's how the
   process usually goes" built from the agent's own experience — clearly experience, not a client claim).

**How [agent] helps next (the skeleton's HOW [AGENT] HELPS NEXT band)** — the soft close in their voice:
their real **buyer offer + USP from `offer.md`**, framed for someone arriving from out of town. **Use ONLY
services actually listed there** — never add relocation extras the offer doesn't contain (virtual showings,
scouting trips, a "relocation concierge"); if nothing in the offer is remote-specific, describe their normal
buyer process and say plainly that it works at a distance. Where to find them per house rules #4 (socials,
website/email — **no call booking, no booking link**).

**Before writing:** skim the top 3 results for *"moving to [City]"* (house rules #8) so pages 4–5 say what
those leave out. No re-asking the agent — use the intake answers you have.

## The two magnet shapes — SECOND campaign onward (SKILL.md Phase 1 picks one)
- **A. Brand-led (general) — the default for most agents.** ONE comprehensive guide for the agent's **whole
  audience and full offer suite** (buyers AND sellers), built around their personal brand. Core promise
  example: *"Everything you need to buy or sell in [city] with confidence."* It still carries **ONE clear
  brand-level promise** — that promise becomes the funnel headline.
- **B. Audience-specific (niche) — when the agent has one dominant focus.** A focused guide matched to their
  strongest reader + offer — pick one type from the table below. An audience is always a **kind of move**,
  never a protected class or family type (house rules #5).

## Audience-specific magnet types (shape B — pick one audience)
| Type | Core promise example |
|---|---|
| First-time buyer guide | "Everything a first-time [city] buyer needs — from pre-approval to keys, in plain English." |
| New-build buyer guide | "How to buy a new build in [city] without overpaying — incentives, builders, and the fine print." |
| Feeder-specific relocation guide *(campaign two+ only — a sharper cut of the locked first guide, never a second general one)* | "Moving from [Place] to [city]: what changes, what it costs, and which communities fit your budget and commute." |
| Relocation guide *(normally campaign one, locked — offer here ONLY if no relocation guide exists yet, e.g. the agent declined it twice)* | "Moving to [city]? Start Here." — use the relocation structure above |
| Seller's pricing/prep guide | "What your [city] home is really worth — and the prep that adds the most before you list." |
| Downsizer / move-up seller guide | "Selling and buying at once in [city]: how to time it without owning two homes." |
| Investor guide | "Cash-flow vs. appreciation in [city]: where the numbers actually work right now." |

For a niche magnet, pick the type that matches the agent's **offer + strongest reader** — it speaks to one
person. A brand-led magnet speaks to the whole audience but still carries ONE clear brand-level promise.
Either way, one promise is what lets the funnel's copy land.

## What makes a magnet worth opting in for
- **Solves one specific, real problem** the reader is actively worried about (overpaying, mispricing, the
  unknown of a new city). Not "10 tips" — *the* answer to *their* question.
- **Genuinely useful — never a tease.** Give the real content. A magnet that's all hype and no substance
  burns trust (and the funnel's promise). Deliver value worth far more than an email.
- **Local and specific.** Real neighbourhoods, real price bands, real steps, real (sourced) numbers. This is
  the agent's local authority on a page — the thing a generic download can't fake.
- **Quick to consume.** A focused guide (5–9 short pages) beats a 40-page doc nobody finishes.
- **Naturally leads to the agent.** Because it's built from the offer, the obvious next step *is* working
  with them — without a single pushy line.

## Guide structure (the generic shape — 5–9 body pages)
Bookends are the skeleton's THE PROMISE and HOW [AGENT] HELPS NEXT bands; the 5–9 pages are the body:
1. **The framing page** — name the problem and the stakes, and promise the way through. Short.
2. **The value pages (the body)** — the actual content, one clear idea per page: the steps, the checklist,
   the numbers, the local specifics, the mistakes to avoid. This is the meat — make it real.
3. **The "what good looks like" page** — a brief, concrete example or mini case (real proof if available).

**The promise** (cover): title + one-line promise + who it's for — becomes the funnel headline.
**How [agent] helps next**: soft close in their voice — where they are now → the agent makes it easy →
where to reach them. **No call booking, no booking link** — a warm "reach out anytime / here's where to find me."

## Tie it to the offer (this is the point)
The magnet and the offer are the same story at two stages: the magnet solves the first problem for free; the
offer is the full solution. Build the magnet so the reader finishes it already wanting what the agent sells —
then the funnel just makes it easy to raise their hand.

## Honesty + compliance
Real, sourced numbers and real proof only — never invent a stat, an incentive, a figure, or a testimonial;
unsourced → qualitative. No guarantees of price/savings/sale. Fair-Housing-safe (the place, never the people).
Run it through `compliance.md` per house rules #5 (the three states).
