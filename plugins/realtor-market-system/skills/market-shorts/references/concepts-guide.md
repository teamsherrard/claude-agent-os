# Concepts Guide — the three market shorts

Three formats, one month's data. Each one is aimed at a different viewer and opens a different way.

---

## Concept 1 — Green screen article reaction

### Purpose
The agent reacts to a real news story about their market, adding the local interpretation that turns
a headline into something useful. Green screen is the strongest format for reaching **locals** rather
than other agents — the timeliness signals "I'm on top of what's happening here right now."

### Choosing the article
Published in the **last 0–7 days** (0–3 is ideal; up to 14 only for rate or policy news with nothing
newer). It must be about one of:
- This month's board release or the price/inventory data itself
- The rate or mortgage environment
- A local development, policy, or infrastructure story that moves the market

It needs at least one specific number the agent can react to. **Fetch it and confirm it loads** —
no paywall, no 404, no redirect to a generic homepage. A headline is not a source.

### Why talking points, not a script
Reactions have to sound unrehearsed. The agent reads the **hook** exactly, then riffs from bullets in
their own words. Faster to film, far more watchable than a script read to camera.

### Structure
```
HOOK (read word-for-word, ≤12 words):
[the number or the claim — city-specific]

TALKING POINTS (riff, don't read):
• What happened — the number, the source, when
• What that actually means for a buyer in [city] right now
• What it means for a seller
• The take — what they're telling their own clients this week
• CTA beat

[ON SCREEN: article headline held up / key stat]
Runtime: 30–60 sec
```

**Banned:** "according to this article," "experts say," "reports indicate." The agent is the expert;
they're telling you, not relaying.

## Concept 2 — Talking-head opinion

### Purpose
Pure connection. No article, no graphic — the agent's genuine read on what the month means. This is
the format that makes people feel like they know them.

### Choosing the angle
Look at what this month's data actually shows and pick the strongest single take. It has to be
specific to *these* numbers — a take that would have worked last month is the wrong take.

| Angle | Use when | Example opening |
|---|---|---|
| **Contrarian** | The data contradicts the popular assumption | *"Everyone keeps telling me it's a terrible time to buy here. June's numbers say something else."* |
| **Before / after** | A meaningful change from 12 months ago | *"A buyer in [city] today has a completely different job than a buyer did last summer."* |
| **Myth buster** | A common local belief the data disproves | *"The thing I hear most right now is [X]. The numbers don't back that up."* |
| **Prediction** | A trend with real momentum — **labelled as opinion** | *"Based on where inventory is heading, here's what I think the next 90 days look like."* |
| **Community spotlight** | One area behaving unlike the rest | *"[Community] is doing something the rest of [city] isn't."* |
| **Direct advice** | The data points to one clear move | *"If I were selling in [city] this month, here's exactly what I'd do first."* |

Best raw material: the property-type divergence, the odd community, or the recurring worry from the
data block's "what people are asking" list.

### Structure — word-for-word
```
HOOK (3–5 sec): [the opinion, stated flat and confident]
CONTEXT (5–10 sec): [the number that backs it]
THE TAKE (25–40 sec): [2–3 beats. Specific. Local. Something they'd actually say to a client]
CTA (5–10 sec): [one action]
```

Scripted, not bulleted — an opinion has to land tight, and a rambling take is a dead post.

## Concept 3 — Stat graphic

### Purpose
Built for the scroll. One number, big enough to read at a glance, that works with the sound off and
pays off when it's turned on. The most shareable of the three and the best fit for stories.

### Choosing the number
The single most arresting stat of the month. Best candidates: the biggest YoY swing · the number that
crosses a threshold (a market flipping from balanced to buyer's) · the affordability figure · the
outlier community. Say why it's the one.

### The design brief (paste-ready)
1080×1920, 9:16. Brand colours and fonts from `identity/brand-visual.md`; if unset, say so and
specify near-black on off-white with one accent.

```
PANEL 1 (0–1s)  — the number, enormous, centred. Nothing else but the accent rule under it.
PANEL 2 (1–3s)  — the label under the number ("days on market, June") + the comparison line
                  ("↑ 7 days vs last June")
PANEL 3 (3–5s)  — the one-line meaning, smaller, lower third
FOOTER          — agent handle + city, small, consistent placement
```

Real values in the brief, panel by panel. Never "insert the stat here."

### The hook rule that matters
The graphic already shows the number. **The hook must not read it out.** The graphic shows *what*;
the hook says *why you should care*.

- Weak: *"Days on market in Calgary hit 32."* (the screen already said that)
- Strong: *"This one number is why your neighbour's house is still sitting."*

### Structure
```
[ON SCREEN: the number]
HOOK (word-for-word, ≤12 words): [why it matters — not the number itself]
VOICEOVER (20–40 sec):
  • the number and its comparison
  • what it means for one specific viewer
  • the one move to make
CTA: [one action]
```

If the month has a second strong number, note it as a swap so the agent can shoot two in one sitting.

## Hook formulas (pick a different one for each concept)

1. **The number that shouldn't be** — *"[City] homes are selling slower than they did in a pandemic."*
2. **The direct address** — *"If you're selling in [city] this month, this one's for you."*
3. **The correction** — *"Everyone's got this backwards right now."*
4. **The threshold** — *"[City] just crossed into a buyer's market. First time in [X]."*
5. **The comparison** — *"Same house, same street, $[X] a month more than last year."*
6. **The consequence** — *"This one number is why your neighbour's house is still sitting."*
7. **The question they're already asking** — *"Should you wait for rates to drop? Here's the math."*
8. **The local secret** — *"[Community] is doing something the rest of [city] isn't."*

---

## Variety rules (check all three together before delivering)

- [ ] Three different hook formulas — no two open the same way
- [ ] Three different viewers — buyer, seller, general local or relocating
- [ ] No two lead with the same stat
- [ ] Three different caption openings
- [ ] Together they tell a fuller story of the month than any one alone
- [ ] None repeats an angle already in `memory/content-log.md`
