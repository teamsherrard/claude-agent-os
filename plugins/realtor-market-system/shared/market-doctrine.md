# Market Doctrine — how this system thinks about a monthly market update

*The methodology every skill in this plugin applies. Aligned with the YouTube Doctrine §9 (Market
Updates) — this file is the market-specific expansion of it. Treat it as doctrine, not one option.*

---

## 1. Why the market update is the highest-leverage video an agent makes

It is the one piece of content that simultaneously proves local authority, answers a question people
actively search every month, and produces **seller** leads (the hardest kind to earn). Most agents
never make it, because pulling the data and building the visual is the hard part. This system does
both, so the agent's only job is to film.

Because it repeats monthly, it compounds: same structure, new numbers, a growing library, and a
search footprint that owns "[city] real estate market [month] [year]" over time.

---

## 2. The timing rule (get this right or nothing else matters)

**Record in the first week of the month, using the PREVIOUS month's COMPLETE data, but title it with
the CURRENT month.**

- Board releases lag: a full month of stats isn't published until the first days of the following
  month. Waiting for "this month's" data means waiting forever.
- Recorded in the first week of July → built on June's complete data → titled **"… July 2026."**
  That matches what viewers are searching *now*, which is where the traffic is.
- Inside the piece, always state the period the data covers ("here are the numbers that just closed
  out June"). The title serves search; the body serves honesty. Both are satisfied.
- **Never use partial-month data as a headline figure.** An incomplete month is not a month.

---

## 3. Interpret, don't recap (the single biggest quality lever)

A stat read aloud is a data read-out. A stat plus its meaning is advice. Every number in every
deliverable gets the second half.

| Recap (wrong) | Interpret (right) |
|---|---|
| "Days on market was 32." | "Homes are taking 32 days — a week longer than last spring. Sellers who price at last year's number are the ones sitting." |
| "Inventory is up 14%." | "There are 14% more homes to choose from than a year ago. If you're buying, this is the most leverage you've had in two years." |
| "The benchmark price is $587,400." | "The typical home is $587,400 — up 2% on the year. Prices are firming, not spiking. That's a normal market, not a runaway one." |

If a line could be pasted into any city's update without changing, it's a recap. Rewrite it.

---

## 4. The three audience lenses (mandatory)

Every report and every script covers all three. This is what turns one recording into every lead type:

1. **Local buyers** — competition level, what their money buys, what to do about rates, one concrete move.
2. **Local sellers** — realistic pricing, how long it takes, demand level, one concrete move.
3. **People relocating in** — what the numbers mean to someone who doesn't know the city yet: what a
   budget actually buys, which areas are moving, what surprises newcomers. This lens is the one most
   agents skip and the one that pulls the highest-intent out-of-town leads.

---

## 5. The 8 headline metrics (pull all of these)

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Benchmark / average / median price | The most-watched number — buyers and sellers lead with it |
| YoY price change | Context: is this higher or lower than a year ago? |
| Total sales volume | How active is the market right now? |
| YoY sales change | Momentum: is activity building or fading? |
| New listings | Supply entering the market this month |
| Active inventory | Total supply available right now |
| Days on market | How fast homes sell — the clearest demand signal |
| Months of supply | The single best indicator of market condition |

**Secondary (pull if published):** sale-to-list ratio · property-type breakdowns (detached, condo,
townhouse) · absorption rate · new-construction starts and incentives · rental vacancy and rates.

---

## 6. Market condition, from months of supply

| Months of supply | Condition |
|---|---|
| Under 2 | Strong seller's market |
| 2–3 | Seller's market |
| 3–4 | Balanced (slight seller favour) |
| 4–6 | Balanced |
| 6+ | Buyer's market |

State the condition in plain words once per deliverable, and make sure every interpretation in that
piece is consistent with it.

---

## 7. Freshness rules

| Data age | Status |
|---|---|
| The just-closed month's board release | Ideal — this is the target |
| One month older (release not out yet) | Acceptable — state the period clearly |
| 2+ months old | Context and comparison only, never a headline figure |
| Forecasts and predictions | Always attributed to a named source, never stated as fact |

If the board release hasn't dropped yet, say so and offer to run it in a few days rather than
shipping stale headline numbers.

## 8. Affordability — make the money real

Percentages don't land; monthly payments do. Every update converts rates into dollars.

**Approximate monthly payment** = (price × (1 − down payment %)) amortized over 25 years (Canada) or
30 years (US) at the current posted rate.

Standard examples to include:
- An entry-level home in this market at minimum down payment
- A mid-range home at 20% down
- Where useful, the same home at last year's rate, so the change is visible

Format: `$[price] home | [X]% down | [X]% rate | ≈ $[monthly]/month`

Label these as approximate and note that they exclude taxes, insurance, and fees.

---

## 9. The 2-CTA model

Two calls to action, never one, never five:

- **Primary CTA** — right after the hook, while attention is highest. The agent's main offer plus
  the booking link, in one natural line.
- **Secondary CTA** — in the outro, after the value has been delivered. Usually the lead magnet
  ("get next month's report by email") or the consult.

The lead magnet CTA is the highest-converting one in a market update, because the report the viewer
just watched *is* the magnet. Point at it every month.

---

## 10. The eight common mistakes (check every deliverable against these)

1. Stats without interpretation — the number read aloud and nothing else.
2. Stale data, or partial-month data used as a headline.
3. Predictions stated as fact, or any guarantee.
4. Overhyping — "the market is exploding," "prices are collapsing."
5. Fear without guidance — naming a scary trend and offering no move to make.
6. Ignoring differences by price point and property type, so the take is true for nobody.
7. Talking to other agents instead of to consumers (jargon, industry shorthand, board acronyms).
8. Forgetting the CTA, or burying it at the very end only.
