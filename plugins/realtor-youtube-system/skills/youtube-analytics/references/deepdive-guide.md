# YouTube Deep Dive — the monthly full breakdown (structure + method)

The deepest thing the YouTube System does: the agent's whole channel, their competitors, the gaps between
them, and a 30-day plan — once a month. Same 4-part shape as the Short-Form deep dive, so one ritual covers
both. Judged against the doctrine throughout (§23 analytics, §24.5 audit, §3 S.E.A.R.C.H., §16–§18
packaging, §22 mix, §15.3 cadence). Rendered on the **Deep Dive Report skeleton** in `shared/doc-format.md`
— a flagship deliverable, stamped `Powered by Mike Sherrard Coaching Inc Frameworks`.

**Window:** default the last 90 days (a month in = "since last dive"). State it in one line, then go.
**Data ladder (best-first, never blocked):** live data connection (recipes 1/2/3/5 — channel stats, full
catalog, per-video views/likes/length/date, competitor pulls, gap search) → the agent's YouTube Studio
export/screenshot for private depth (CTR, avg view duration, retention, traffic sources, search terms) →
public channel reads for anything else. Say which sources you used in the report header.

---

## Part 1 — The channel audit (their own YouTube, in full)

### 1a. Growth & reach
Subscribers now vs. the count stored at the last dive (`memory/performance.md`) → real growth, not a
point-in-time number. Total views in the window, videos published, average views per video vs. the
channel's OWN median (never generic benchmarks). New/small channel (≤5 videos)? Say "too early to read
trends" and keep the audit to packaging + plan.

### 1b. Content inventory (every video, ranked & tagged)
List every video in the window, tagged by **pillar** and **content type** (the 6: market update · home tour
· relocation · community tour · map tour · local lifestyle — from the Game Plan / Videos folders /
content-log), with views, length, publish date, and — when Studio data is in — CTR + average view duration.
Then the averages **per content type** and **per pillar**: which lane is pulling, which is dead weight.
Classify each video by intent (§23.4): awareness · trust-building · high-intent lead-gen · niche authority ·
underperformer needing a new title/thumbnail · repeat-from-a-new-angle.

### 1c. Packaging & hook read (§16–§17, the CTR/retention edge)
- **Packaging:** with Studio data, CTR per video → which titles/thumbnails earn the click. Without it, read
  views-vs-median as the proxy. Check the title gates (market in the title · one promise · ≤70 chars ·
  search language) and the §17 thumbnail rules per type — name the 3 best-packaged videos and the 3 that
  need a re-title/re-thumbnail (§23.4).
- **Hook & retention:** average view duration / % viewed vs. length → **hook vs. middle**: low % viewed
  with a fine CTR = the first 30–60s (§5); a good open that sags = the middle (§7, too long/no payoff).
  Quote the 3 best openings verbatim and say why each held.

### 1d. The funnel leak (§23.2 — leads, not views)
Walk **impressions → clicks (CTR) → watch time → the CTA → leads**: which videos produced comments, calls,
booked appointments (ask the agent; the §23.2 question — "which video made you reach out?" — is the truest
data). Name the **single biggest leak** plainly (*"strong views on relocation, no booked calls — the primary
CTA is buried at the end; move it to right after the hook, §6.1"*).

### 1e. Cadence & consistency (§15.3, §22.4)
Videos/week vs. the plan (2/wk ideal · 1/wk minimum); gaps and streaks; market update in week one each
month (§9.3)? Pillar coverage vs. the Game Plan's 3 pillars — which pillar is starving.

## Part 2 — The competitor breakdown (YouTube — deep, public data, full support)
Identify **3–5 competitors:** `identity/strategy.md` (competitors they admire) + `identity/market.md`; if
thin, ask once for a couple of channels; optionally discover local ones (search "[city] realtor",
"moving to [city]"). Per competitor (recipes 1–2): channel stats → their uploads → per-video details. Then read:
- **Outliers** — videos whose views are a multiple of that channel's OWN median = topics proven to work; a
  small **local** channel overperforming counts double (that proves the topic works *here*).
- Their top ~10 topics, title patterns, thumbnail style, cadence, and content-type mix.
- **What they do that the agent doesn't** — the transferable moves — and **what the agent does better**.
- The positioning verdict: where the agent sits in the market's YouTube landscape, in two sentences.

---

## Part 3 — Gaps & opportunities
- **Content-gap read** (recipe 3 logic): local topics with real demand + weak/stale/non-local coverage →
  specific bets with the evidence attached (*"'moving to [city] 2027' — top result is 18 months old, no
  local agent covers it"*). Never dress result counts up as "searches."
- **Pillar gaps:** what the Game Plan planned vs. what got filmed — the starving pillar is usually the
  opportunity.
- **Repeat-from-a-new-angle plays (§3 R):** the agent's own winners that deserve 2–3 more angles.
- **Packaging fixes worth doing this week:** the underperformers a new title/thumbnail could revive.

---

## Part 4 — Synthesis + the next-30-days plan
- **Top 3 strengths to double down on** — each with the number that proves it.
- **Top 3 fixes** — the leaks/weaknesses, each with the specific change (a rewritten title, a moved CTA, a
  new thumbnail direction — not "improve your hooks").
- **The 30-day plan:** ~8 videos at 2/wk (or 4 at 1/wk), following the §22 mix, seeded from the winners +
  the gaps + the new-angle plays — exact titles (title gates apply), content type, and the week. Detailed
  enough to hand straight to *"make this video"*. Offer to start the first one now.
- **One sentence:** the single most important move this month.

---

## Honesty rules (non-negotiable)
- Every number real and labelled by source; empties called unavailable; **no invented benchmarks** — the
  agent's own median is the benchmark.
- Private metrics (CTR, retention, traffic sources) appear ONLY when the agent supplied Studio data — say so
  when they didn't, and say what it would unlock.
- Thin data is called thin. One video is never a trend.
- Competitor Instagram/TikTok are out of scope here (that's the Short-Form dive) — YouTube only, done deep.
- Read-only always: nothing is ever posted, edited, or changed on any channel.
