# Metrics Guide — reading YouTube data like a coach, and saying it like a friend

The job isn't to report numbers — it's to tell the agent what's working, what isn't, and *why*, then what
to do. Judged against the doctrine (§23), always through to **leads**, not views. And said in words a
realtor who has never opened YouTube Studio understands.

## The plain names (use these in the report — the first time a number appears, add the meaning)

| Number | What to call it | The one-line meaning to put next to it |
|---|---|---|
| Impressions | "how often YouTube showed your thumbnail" | Studio only. Is the topic being shown to anyone? |
| CTR | "click-through rate" | Of the people who saw your thumbnail, how many clicked. Your title + thumbnail verdict. |
| Views | "views" | People who watched. Read against your typical video, never against a big channel. |
| Median views | "your typical video" | Half your videos do better than this, half worse — the fair benchmark. |
| Average view duration / % viewed | "watch time" | How long people stay. 40%+ on a 12-minute video is strong. |
| Retention graph | "where people leave" | Studio only. A cliff at 0:30 = the opening; a slow slide = the middle drags. |
| Traffic sources | "how people found you" | Studio only. Search vs browse vs suggested — is the search strategy working? |
| Search terms | "what people typed to find you" | Studio only. Free keyword research; terms you don't own yet = new videos. |
| Demographics / geography | "who's watching" | Studio only. Age, gender, top locations — are these local buyers? |
| Subscribers gained | "new subscribers" | Trust at scale — only worth what it turns into. Not the number to chase (§2.4). |
| Comments / comment rate | "comments" · "comments per 100 views" | People talking back. Questions = free video ideas; buyer intent = leads in public. |
| Likes | "likes" | Mild approval. Rarely decides anything. |
| Publish day / hour | "when you post" | Which day and hour your typical views are highest. |
| Tags | "tags" | Barely matter (Mike's rule). Note present/absent, move on. |
| Chapters | "chapters (the timestamps in the description)" | Help people skip to what they came for — and help search. |
| Captions (uploaded) | "captions" | Uploaded subtitles (auto-captions don't count). Helps search and silent viewers. |
| Description first 3 lines | "the top of your description" | What shows before "more": the booking link + free guide belong here. |
| Playlists / sections | "your channel page" | Do the playlists match your 3 pillars? Is the homepage laid out? |
| Search rank | "where you show up" | Your position when someone types the phrase into YouTube. |
| AI answer presence | "whether an AI assistant mentions you" | When someone asks an AI assistant the buyer's question, are you in the answer? |
| Leads / calls booked | "leads" · "calls booked" | **The business outcome.** The §23.2 question — "which video made you reach out?" — is the truest data. |

## What each metric actually tells you (and the fix when it's low)

| Metric | Source | What it really means | If it's low… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Impressions** | Studio pack | Is the topic being surfaced? | Topic not searched / too broad / no keywords — check the title against §16 + §18.3. |
| **Click-through rate** | Studio pack | **The packaging verdict** (title + thumbnail). Benchmark = the channel's own median, not a "4–8%" myth. | Re-title (market in it, one promise, ≤70 chars) and/or re-thumbnail (§17). Cheapest fix on YouTube — do it to old videos too. |
| **Views** | live | Surfacing × clicking. Read vs the channel's median. | Usually packaging or topic — look at impressions + CTR before blaming the content. |
| **Watch time / % viewed** | Studio pack | Did people *stay* — the truest quality signal. | Low with a fine CTR = the **hook** (first 30–60 s, §5) or the **middle** (§7). |
| **Retention graph** | Studio pack | *Where* they leave. | Cut what's before the cliff; open on the viewer's question, never "welcome back." |
| **Traffic sources** | Studio pack | Search vs browse vs suggested — is S.E.A.R.C.H. working? | Little search traffic = titles aren't matching real queries; check §16.4. |
| **Search terms** | Studio pack | Free keyword research. | Terms don't match the market → titles are off; terms you don't own yet → new video ideas. |
| **Demographics / geography** | Studio pack | Are these local buyers and sellers? | Out-of-market or wrong-age audience → topics too broad; go local (community, map, relocation). |
| **Packaging audit** (title gates · description top · chapters · captions · publish time) | live (recipe 1) | Is each video dressed to be found and clicked? | Fix in this order: title → description top → chapters → captions. Publish at the best day/hour. |
| **Comments** | live (may be blocked) / screenshots | Engagement + questions = free ideation + leads in public. | No question asked; no pinned comment; the ask didn't invite conversation. |
| **Search rank / AI presence** | live (recipe 7) | Do you show up when someone looks? | The phrase isn't in a title; the About page / site doesn't state the market; no page per community. |
| **Subscribers gained** | live | Trust at scale. | A one-off with no series; no playlist path (Shift 4). |
| **Leads / calls booked** | the agent | **The business outcome.** | The ask: buried, generic, or missing — primary ask right after the hook (§6.1), secondary at the end (§6.5). |

## How to diagnose *why* a video under- or over-performed
Walk the path in order — the first weak link is usually the cause. Say it in plain words:
1. **Few people saw it?** → the **topic/title** isn't being searched (or is too broad). Fix the search intent.
2. **Seen, but not clicked?** → **packaging**. Title + thumbnail. Nothing downstream matters if nobody clicks.
3. **Clicked, but left early?** → the **opening** (a cliff in the first minute) or the **middle** (a slow slide).
4. **Watched, but no comments or subscribers?** → no reason to engage — no question, no series, no next video.
5. **Engaged, but no leads?** → the **ask** is weak, late, or not tied to a real free guide.

**With the Studio pack you can settle opening-vs-middle exactly:** a retention cliff before 1:00 = the
opening (rewrite it per §5.4 — call out the viewer, the tension, the promise, a reason to stay); a steady
slide after a strong open = the middle (one idea per section, get to the payoff, cut length — §7, §27.6).

**Without the Studio pack** (live only): views vs the channel's median + length is your proxy. Say so. Never
invent CTR or retention numbers — offer the "add my Studio numbers" path that unlocks them.

## Classify by intent, not just performance (§23.4)
A low-view video can still be a win. Tag each: **awareness** · **trust-building** · **high-intent lead-gen**
· **niche authority** · **underperformer → new title/thumbnail** · **worth repeating from a new angle**.
A relocation video with 600 views that booked two calls outranks a 10k-view lifestyle video that booked none.

## Turn it into advice (always — in the four-line shape)
Every finding: **what we found → why it matters to you → do this → the proof.** Tie it to their actual
videos and offer to act:
- *What we found:* your relocation videos pull 3× your typical video. *Why it matters:* that's the lane
  bringing you the people who are actually moving. *Do this:* two more relocation angles next month —
  "Moving to Calgary in 2027? Watch This First" and "The 5 Calgary Neighbourhoods Newcomers Regret."
  *The proof:* 4,800 and 3,900 views vs a typical 1,400.
- *What we found:* your market updates get shown but not clicked. *Do this:* the rewrite — "Calgary Housing
  Market Update October 2026: Are Prices Finally Dropping?" *The proof:* 2.1% click-through vs your 4%.

## Honesty rules
- The agent's own median is the benchmark — never a generic industry number.
- Thin data is called thin. One video is never a trend; a spike can be a fluke; a day-old video is not a verdict.
- Private metrics appear only from the Studio pack — say what's missing and what it unlocks.
- Never invent or estimate a number. If it isn't there, say "not available" and how to add it.
- YouTube compounds slowly — a quiet month isn't a failure; keep them consistent (§3 "H").
