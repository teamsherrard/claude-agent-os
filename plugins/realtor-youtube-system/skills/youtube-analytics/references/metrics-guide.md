# Metrics Guide — reading YouTube data like a coach

The job isn't to report numbers — it's to tell the agent what's working, what isn't, and *why*, then what
to do. Judged against the doctrine (§23), always through to **leads**, not views.

## What each metric actually tells you

| Metric | Source | What it really means | If it's low… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Impressions** | Studio | How often YouTube showed the thumbnail — is the topic being surfaced? | Topic not searched / too broad / no keywords — check the title against §16 + §18.3. |
| **Click-through rate (CTR)** | Studio | Of those who saw it, who clicked — **the packaging verdict** (title + thumbnail). Benchmark = the channel's own median, not a "4–8%" myth. | Re-title (market in it, one promise, ≤70 chars) and/or re-thumbnail (§17 per-type rules). Cheapest fix on YouTube — do it to old videos too. |
| **Views** | public / live | The combined result of surfacing × clicking. Read it vs. the channel's median. | Usually packaging or topic — look at impressions + CTR before blaming the content. |
| **Average view duration / % viewed** | Studio | Did people *stay* — the truest quality signal. Read against length: 40%+ on a 12-min video is strong. | Low with a fine CTR = the **hook** (first 30–60s, §5) or the **middle** (§7). See the diagnosis below. |
| **Retention graph** | Studio | *Where* they leave. A cliff at 0:30 = the intro; a slow slide = pacing; a spike = a moment worth repeating. | Cut what's before the cliff; open on the viewer's question, never "welcome back." |
| **Traffic sources** | Studio | Search vs. browse vs. suggested — is the S.E.A.R.C.H. strategy working? | Little search traffic = titles aren't matching real queries; check §16.4 (beat the ranking videos). |
| **Search terms** | Studio | The exact phrases that found them — free keyword research. | Terms don't match the market → titles are off; terms you don't own yet → new video ideas. |
| **Subscribers gained** | public / live | Trust at scale — but only worth what it converts. | A one-off with no series; no playlist path (Shift 4). Not the metric to chase (§2.4). |
| **Comments** | public / live | Engagement + questions = free ideation + leads in public (→ `youtube-comments`). | No question asked; no pinned comment; CTA didn't invite conversation. |
| **Leads / calls booked** | the agent | **The business outcome.** The §23.2 question ("which video made you reach out?") is the truest data the channel has. | The CTA: buried, generic, or missing — primary CTA belongs right after the hook (§6.1), secondary at the end (§6.5). |

## How to diagnose *why* a video under- or over-performed
Walk the funnel in order — the first weak link is usually the cause:
1. **Low impressions?** → the **topic/title** isn't being searched (or is too broad). Fix the search intent.
2. **Impressions fine, low CTR?** → **packaging**. Title + thumbnail. Nothing downstream matters if nobody clicks.
3. **Good CTR, low % viewed?** → the **hook** (cliff in the first minute) or the **middle** (slow slide).
4. **Good watch time, no comments/subs?** → no reason to engage — no question, no series, no next-video path.
5. **Good engagement, no leads?** → the **CTA** is weak, late, or not tied to a real lead magnet.

Name the specific weak link in plain words — that's the insight a dashboard can't give.

**With Studio data you can settle hook-vs-middle exactly:** a retention cliff before 1:00 = the hook
(rewrite the open per §5.4 — call out the viewer, the tension, the promise, a reason to stay); a steady slide
after a strong open = the middle (one idea per section, get to the payoff, cut length — §7, §27.6).

**Without Studio data** (public/live only): views vs. the channel's median + length is your proxy. Say so.
Never invent CTR or retention numbers — offer the screenshot path that unlocks them.

## Classify by intent, not just performance (§23.4)
A low-view video can still be a win. Tag each: **awareness** · **trust-building** · **high-intent lead-gen**
· **niche authority** · **underperformer → new title/thumbnail** · **worth repeating from a new angle**.
A relocation video with 600 views that booked two calls outranks a 10k-view lifestyle video that booked none.

## Turn it into advice (always)
Every analysis ends with: **what's working → do more of it**, and **the one weak link → here's the fix.**
Tie it to their actual videos and offer to act:
- "Your relocation videos pull 3× your median — that pillar is your engine. Two more angles on it next month."
- "Your market updates get impressions but a 2% click-through — the titles read like news headlines. Here's
  the rewrite: '[City] Housing Market Update October 2026: Are Prices Finally Dropping?'"
- "Your best hook was the contrarian one ('most people moving here pick the wrong side of town') — use that
  opening style on the next community tour."

## Honesty rules
- The agent's own median is the benchmark — never a generic industry number.
- Thin data is called thin. One video is never a trend; a spike can be a fluke.
- Private metrics appear only when the agent supplied Studio data — say what's missing and what it unlocks.
- Never invent or estimate a number. If it isn't there, say so and ask for the screenshot.
- YouTube compounds slowly — a quiet month isn't a failure; keep them consistent (§3 "H").
