# The hook — open on the strongest line

Viewers decide in the first three seconds. This craft finds the best opening and makes the video start there.

## Find it (free)

Read the first 60 to 90 seconds of the transcript and score each candidate opening line 0 to 2 on: **a claim or a number** · **curiosity** (it opens a question) · **speaks to the viewer** ("you") · **short** (under ~12 words) · **true to the video** (the video actually pays it off). The best hook is usually already in the first minute, just not first.

## Three moves, in order of preference

1. **It is already first** — cut only the settling-in before it (the breath, "okay," the throat-clear, "let's go").
2. **It is in the first minute, after a slow intro** — cut from 0 to the hook's first word with the resolver. Check that what follows still makes sense without the removed intro; if a name or a setup is lost, keep that sentence.
3. **The best line is later in the video** — do NOT move it (reordering is not available on this engine, and teasing a clip out of order needs the agent's say-so). Say it: *"Your strongest line is at 4:10 — 'most agents won't tell you this.' Want to re-record a 5-second open with that line? It would lift the first ten seconds a lot."*

## The hook card

The on-screen hook line is the agent's own opening sentence, tightened, never a new claim: 3 to 6 words on a reel (25 characters or fewer, so it fits the vertical frame), up to ~8 on long-form. It is on-screen text, not a title — the video's title belongs to the YouTube System (`boundaries.md`). Placement and size live in `graphics-style.md`. Compliance first: no superlatives, no guarantees.

## Verify

Re-read the first row after the cut: the first playable word is the hook's first word. On a frame: the first second shows the agent mid-energy, not settling.
