# One Day With Your AI Admin — Video Map (flagship, ~15–18 min)

*Week 9's centerpiece. Not a feature tour — a NARRATED DAY with a clock on screen, then a zoom-out
to the weekly and monthly rhythm. Every demo line below is a real, shipped trigger — type or say it
on camera and it works. Companion pieces: the 20-slide training deck (video 1) and the Rhythm Card
one-pager (appendix below, for Claude Design).*

**Format:** screen recording of Cowork for the typed beats; the dispatch beat is the phone-voice
moment (QuickTime + USB cable, Mac mic as audio source — NOT iPhone Mirroring, which kills the
mic). A clock graphic advances in the corner: 7:00 → 8:30 → 11:15 → 1:55 → 3:40 → 6:00.

**Prep before recording:** run on the demo Brain with 6–8 realistic clients seeded, 2 hot-lead
emails planted in the inbox, one deliberately double-booked slot, one parked capture from
"yesterday," and `identity/business-plan.md` filled in — the TOP 3 beat depends on it.

---

## COLD OPEN (0:00–0:50)

**Say:** "I'm going to run my entire day as a realtor — bookings, inbox, client prep, follow-ups —
and I'm never going to open my calendar, sort my inbox, or write an email from scratch. Once. This
is one day with your AI Admin, and by the end I'll give you the exact daily, weekly, and monthly
rhythm my agents run."

**On screen:** the clock graphic at 6:59am. No UI yet. Tension, not features.

---

## CHAPTER 1 — 7:00am · THE BRIEFING (0:50–3:30)

The briefing has already landed (scheduled task — emphasize nobody asked for it).

**Demo:** open it and read it TOP TO BOTTOM, in its real order:
1. **URGENT** — the hot lead from last night, today's hard deadline.
2. **THE DAY** — every appointment, and pause on the magic: **the prep line under each client
   meeting** (who · where you left off · what to say).
3. **TOP 3 MONEY-MAKING ACTIONS** — say explicitly: "these aren't generic. They're anchored to my
   90-day business plan — the three weekly non-negotiables I set in my Brain."
4. **ON-THE-GO NOTES** — yesterday's parked capture surfaces here: "see this? I mumbled something
   half-finished from the car yesterday. It didn't guess — it parked it and is asking me now."

**Teach: THE MORNING 15.** One graphic: *15 minutes, coffee in hand — read the briefing, send the
drafts, confirm the TOP 3. Your day is now aimed. Most agents spend their first 90 minutes
figuring out what today even is.*

**Tips to drop:**
- "Your briefing time is yours — I do 7, some do 5:30. Say 'change my briefing time.'"
- "The briefing is only as smart as last night's wrap — remember that; it pays off at 6pm."

## CHAPTER 2 — 8:30am · THE INBOX IS ALREADY DONE (3:30–5:30)

The weekday sweep has also already run. Open Gmail on camera.

**Demo:**
1. Show the labels applied: Hot Lead · Active Client · Under Contract · Lender-Title.
2. **The money shot** — the sweep report line: *"3 hot leads — drafts waiting in your Gmail,
   review and send."* Open one draft. Point at the screen: "I did not write this. It's in MY
   voice. That lead emailed at 11pm; the reply was drafted before I woke up. Speed-to-lead is
   how you win clients — review, send, done."
3. Show the WAITING ON section: "…and it's tracking who owes ME replies. Hold that thought —
   that's a Friday thing."

**Tip:** "The sweep never sends and never deletes without permission. Sorted, drafted, reported —
you stay the sender. Every time."

## CHAPTER 3 — THROUGH THE DAY · YOU JUST TALK TO IT (5:30–10:30)

Rapid-fire montage, clock jumping. Keep each beat under 30 seconds — the SPEED is the message.

**11:15am — book it (typed):**
> "Book the Hendersons Saturday at 2 for a showing at 412 Hawkwood."

Read the confirmation aloud: conflict-checked, invite sent, duration pulled from my rules — and
for a virtual consult, the Zoom link just appears. "One line. Proof embedded. I never open the
calendar to double-check."

**11:20am — the conflict (typed):**
> "Push my 3pm to Thursday."

Show it re-checking conflicts before it moves anything.

**1:55pm — in the car, before the 2pm (typed on phone):**
> "Prep me for my 2pm."

Read the 5 lines: WHO · LAST · OPEN · WATCH · SAY. "Thirty seconds at a red light and I walk in
like I remember everything. Because it does."

**3:40pm — THE DISPATCH MOMENT (spoken, phone segment — the wow beat):**
> "Just showed the Andersons 412 Hawkwood — they loved it, some kitchen concerns. Remind me to
> send them comps Thursday, and draft them a thank-you."

One spoken breath → show the report: showing logged · reminder set for Thursday · thank-you draft
in Gmail. "Three tasks, zero taps, driving the whole time. And if it can't figure something out —
a name it doesn't know — it never guesses. It parks it and asks me in tomorrow's briefing. That's
the 10-SECOND DISPATCH RULE: if a thought would take ten seconds to say, say it NOW. Never carry
a thought in your head past the next red light."

**4:15pm — total recall (typed):**
> "What do I know about the Patels?"

**4:16pm — a promise becomes a follow-up (typed):**
> "I told the Patels I'd send them the lender's number by Friday."

Show the dated follow-up land. "Promises made in passing are where deals die. Not anymore."

**4:30pm — THE CROWD-PLEASER (typed):**
> "I'm slammed today."

It reads the actual day and PROPOSES: push the 3pm, draft the Patel reply now, the Lees can wait
till Friday — say go. "It doesn't sympathize and ask questions. It reads my day and hands me the
three offloads. I say 'go.'"

## CHAPTER 4 — 6:00pm · WRAP THE DAY (10:30–12:30)

**Demo (typed or spoken from the driveway):**
> "Wrap my day — Hendersons loved it, offer probably coming. Lees no-showed, want to rebook.
> Patels are ready to write."

Show what one rambling sentence did: three mini-debriefs logged · promises marked done ·
leftovers rolled to tomorrow · then the payoff report: **tomorrow's first appointment (already
prepped) + follow-ups due + ONE first move.**

Then the wrap offers it and you take it:
> "Confirm tomorrow."

Show the confirmation drafts — time, address, what to bring — one per client. "The no-show
killer. Ninety seconds from the driveway, and tomorrow is already confirmed before I walk in the
front door. **This is the habit that makes the whole system compound: tonight's wrap IS
tomorrow's briefing.**"

**Before-you-sleep rule (say it plainly):** wrap the day, tap send on the confirmation drafts,
and put the phone down — the Admin holds the rest until 7am. You're allowed to stop thinking.

## CHAPTER 5 — ZOOM OUT · THE RHYTHM (12:30–16:00)

Clock graphic becomes a calendar. "One day is the demo. The RHYTHM is the system. Here's exactly
what my agents run — daily, weekly, monthly."

**MONDAY (start of week, ~5 min):**
- The briefing's TOP 3 leans on your 90-day plan — Monday is when you believe it.
- Say **"Who owes me?"** — start the week with every dangling ask chased, not discovered Thursday.
- Skim the week ahead; anything wrongly booked, one line fixes it: "push it," "cancel it."

**EVERY MORNING:** the Morning 15 (briefing → send drafts → confirm TOP 3).

**EVERY EVENING:** wrap + confirm tomorrow (Chapter 4). Ten seconds of dispatch all day between.

**FRIDAY (end of week, ~10 min):**
- Say **"Scorecard."** What you kept · what's slipping — each with its fix drafted (say "draft
  them" and Monday's catch-ups are in Gmail) · who went quiet · next week's first move.
- **Clear the parked:** anything still sitting in ON-THE-GO NOTES gets a decision — do it, date
  it, or delete it. Nothing rides into a second week.
- Listing agents: **"Get feedback on [your listing]"** — feedback requests go out while the
  showings are fresh, themes come back for your seller update.

**1st OF THE MONTH (mostly automatic — say so honestly):**
- The briefing quietly does the housekeeping itself: closed and stale clients move to the
  archive, old done items clear out. You do nothing.
- Your 15 minutes: run **"Scorecard"** with fresh month energy, run **"Who owes me?"** deep, and
  ask yourself if the 90-day plan in your Brain is still THE plan — if the quarter turned,
  update it, because your daily TOP 3 flows from it.
- New listing this month? **"Who wants this listing?"** — let Match-Back mine every note you
  logged all month.

**Say:** "Notice what you never saw me do today: open my calendar to check it, sort a single
email, or write anything from scratch. The Admin did the buttons; I did the relationships. Start

your first scorecard on a week the system actually caught. Say 'Set up my AI Admin' and go."

# APPENDIX — THE RHYTHM CARD (one-page handout, give to Claude Design)

**Title:** Your AI Admin Rhythm — print it, live it

**EVERY MORNING — The Morning 15**
Read the briefing top to bottom → send the waiting drafts → confirm your TOP 3. Day aimed.

**ALL DAY — The 10-Second Dispatch Rule**
If a thought takes ten seconds to say, say it NOW — from the car, between showings. Never carry
a thought past the next red light. It logs, reminds, drafts, books. Zero questions.

**BEFORE EVERY MEETING**
"Prep me for my 2pm." Five lines, thirty seconds, walk in warm.

**EVERY EVENING — Wrap + Confirm**
"Wrap my day — [outcomes in one breath]." Then "Confirm tomorrow." Send the drafts, put the
phone down. Tonight's wrap is tomorrow's briefing.

**MONDAY — Aim the Week**
"Who owes me?" — chase every dangling ask before it costs you. Believe your TOP 3.

**FRIDAY — Close the Week**
"Scorecard." Kept · slipping (say "draft them") · gone quiet · first move Monday.
Clear every parked note: do it, date it, or delete it.
Listing agents: "Get feedback on [address]."

**1st OF THE MONTH — 15 Minutes**
Housekeeping runs itself. You: a fresh "Scorecard," a deep "Who owes me?", and check the 90-day
plan in your Brain still holds — your daily TOP 3 flows from it.
New listing? "Who wants this listing?"

**The rule under all of it:** it drafts, you send. It books, you see proof. It remembers, you
show up human.
