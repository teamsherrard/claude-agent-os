## SLIDE 5 — Step 1: Set it up

**ON SLIDE:**
# Step 1
## Type: `"Set up my listing system"`
What happens:
- It reads your Brain. It asks nothing it already knows.
- It locks in your **brokerage disclaimer and licence number** — first.
- It asks five things: your MLS character limit, your farm area, your brokerage's print rules, how you
  publish, and whether you want cinematic tours.
- It tidies up any listings already on file.
Takes about three minutes. **Run it once, ever.**

**SAY:** Do this before your first listing. The disclaimer step matters most — that's what has to appear
on your MLS remarks and anything you mail. If it isn't set, the system leaves a blank for you rather than
guessing. It will never invent a disclaimer and put it on your marketing.

## SLIDE 6 — Step 2: Start a listing

**ON SLIDE:**
# Step 2
## Two ways in — both work
**In the car, one line:**
`"Just listed 14 Oak — start the content"`
→ It takes the address from your sentence and asks three things. Then it starts.

**At your desk:**
`"I just got a listing"`
→ One short form: address, type, beds/baths, price, features, who it's for, the stage, your photos.

**SAY:** Use the one-liner when you're walking out of the appointment. Use the form when you're sitting
down and want it done properly. Either way you answer once. Messy answers are fine — it won't make
anything up to fill a gap, it'll mark it and ask you later.

## SLIDE 8 — WIN IT

**ON SLIDE:**
# WIN IT
## Type: `"I have a listing appointment"`
Before the listing exists:
- The **pre-listing package** you send the night before
- The presentation section by section — with **what to say** on each
- The pricing conversation, scripted honestly
- The four objections that actually lose listings
- The follow-up if they don't sign in the room

**SAY:** Send the package the night before. The appointment goes completely differently when they've
already read why you. And note — it will never invent a price or a CMA. It leaves your number blank,
because that's your analysis, not the computer's.

## SLIDE 9 — LIST IT

**ON SLIDE:**
# LIST IT
## Type: `"Write my MLS description"`
- Sized to **your** MLS's character limit — asked once, remembered forever
- Plus a 500 and a 250 version, written fresh — never chopped off
- Plus a portal version with no contact details, because they strip it
- Character count shown under every version
- Fair-housing checked, hard

**SAY:** This is the piece most likely to get an agent in trouble. No "perfect for families," no "great
schools," no "quiet family neighbourhood" — those are fair housing problems and it rewrites them into
real facts about the house instead.

---

## SLIDE 10 — LAUNCH IT

**ON SLIDE:**
# LAUNCH IT
## Type: `"Posts for my listing"` and `"What ad should I run?"`
**The posts:** coming soon, just listed, feature spotlight, open house invite — plus a price change and
a just sold post, held for later. Five story slides with the sticker for each.
**The ad:** ONE ad recommended, and why. What to spend. What to watch.
Every ad carries the Meta housing-category note — restricted targeting, minimum radius.

**SAY:** It picks one ad and tells you why, instead of listing options. Nine times out of ten it's the
neighbour ad — because that one markets the house *and* wins you the next listing on that street.

---

## SLIDE 11 — FILM IT

**ON SLIDE:**
# FILM IT
## Type: `"Video script for 14 Oak"`
- A 3–5 minute walkthrough script, room by room, written to be **spoken**
- A shot list in **filming order** — so you capture it all in one pass
- The full YouTube package: five titles with character counts, the description, timestamps, 15–20 tags,
  and a thumbnail brief

**SAY:** One breath per line, filmable while you're walking, alone, on your phone. The shot list is the
part that saves you a second trip — it reorders every shot into the order you'd actually walk the house.

---

## SLIDE 12 — TOUR IT

**ON SLIDE:**
# TOUR IT
## Type: `"Animate my listing photos"`
No filming. No videographer.
- Your listing photos become a **cinematic tour** — one clip per room
- Ready for Reels, TikTok, Shorts
- **You approve every clip before anything is used**
- Every tour says it was made from the photos
Runs on your own Higgsfield account.

**SAY:** This is the one that gets the reaction. Every listing gets a tour now, even the ones you never
set foot in with a camera. And the rule it follows is strict — the camera moves, the house doesn't. It
will never add furniture or change a room, because that's misrepresenting somebody's house.

---

## SLIDE 13 — CLIP IT

**ON SLIDE:**
# CLIP IT
## Type: `"Shorts for my listing"`
Three different shorts, filmable in one trip:
1. **Just listed** — the announcement
2. **Top 3 features** — the one that gets saved and sent
3. **The neighbourhood** — reaches people who aren't buying this house
One combined shot list. A caption for each.

**SAY:** Three genuinely different angles — not the same script three times. The neighbourhood one is the
sleeper: most of your audience isn't buying that house, but they all live near it.

---

## SLIDE 14 — SHOW IT

**ON SLIDE:**
# SHOW IT
## Type: `"Open house kit"`
Everything, timed backward from your date:
- 5 days out: the invite · 4 days: the **neighbour invite** · 3 days: agents · 2 days: your buyers
- Day of: five story lines, timed to the hours
- The sign-in question that gets a real answer
- After: four follow-ups — including **the update to your seller**

**SAY:** Text the neighbours yourself. That's the one that fills the room, and it's the one that turns
into your next listing on that street. And the seller update that night is the message most agents forget.

---

## SLIDE 15 — SERVE IT

**ON SLIDE:**
# SERVE IT
## Type: `"Log a showing"` · `"Weekly seller update"` · `"Marketing report"`
- Tell it what people said — it files the feedback with a running count
- The **weekly seller update**: what happened, what people said, what you did, what's next
- The **marketing report**: everything you've done for that listing, item by item, with numbers

**SAY:** This is the retention stage. Listings aren't lost when they expire — they're lost in week three
when the seller decides nobody's working. Sellers rate you on communication more than results. This is
the only part of the system whose whole job is keeping the seller.

---

## SLIDE 16 — REVIVE IT

**ON SLIDE:**
# REVIVE IT
## Type: `"It's not selling"`
It gives you the **honest read** first — and there are only three answers:
- Showings but no offers → the price, or a fixable objection
- Barely any showings → the price band, or your photos
- Normal activity, inside the average → **be patient.** Nothing's wrong.
Then: the price conversation, the re-launch, and the expiry conversation **before** the expiry.

**SAY:** Notice the third answer. If your listing is at 18 days in a 30-day market, it will tell you to
wait — it won't sell you a price reduction to look busy. That honesty is the point. And it never invents
the new number; that's your comps and your call.

---

## SLIDE 17 — SELL IT

**ON SLIDE:**
# SELL IT
## Type: `"We have multiple offers"`
- The offer-deadline post
- **Final calls to every agent who showed** — the message that moves the number
- The multiple-offer process checklist — for you, not to send
- The under-bidder follow-up — the warmest buyer list that exists
It will never tell you which offer to take.

**SAY:** Four minutes texting the agents who showed is worth more than anything else you do that day. And
be clear on the boundary — it writes what you say, it does not weigh offers. That's you and your
brokerage.

---

## SLIDE 18 — CLOSE IT

**ON SLIDE:**
# CLOSE IT
## Type: `"It closed"`
- The sold post — opens on the result, closes on the neighbour line
- The client story — told as **your seller's** win, not yours
- The review request, timed to 2–4 days after closing, with one follow-up
- The thank-you note
- Your result written into your **proof file** — so your next listing presentation is stronger

**SAY:** Send the thank-you the day it closes and the review ask three days later. That order is the whole
trick. And every closing makes your next listing presentation better, because the result gets saved.

## SLIDE 20 — Your listing board

**ON SLIDE:**
# Type: `"Build my listing board"`
Every listing as a card in your own Notion:
- What stage it's at · what's been built · **what's next**
- **Needs Attention** — the open house in four days with no invite out
- **Seller Check-in** — anyone you haven't updated in a week
- Your sold listings become your track record

**SAY:** If you've got one listing, you don't need this. At three or four you do — that's the point where
agents start dropping balls. Ask it "what needs attention?" and it tells you the one thing to do today.

## SLIDE 21 — Let it schedule

**ON SLIDE:**
# Type: `"Schedule my listing posts"`
- It lays your launch across the real days — front-loaded to the first 72 hours
- Or works backward from your open house date
- It shows you the whole queue and waits for your yes

Needs Metricool, GoHighLevel, or Buffer — your own account.

**SAY:** Nothing goes out without you seeing it and saying yes. No connector? It hands you copy-paste-ready

## SLIDE 22 — The morning watch

**ON SLIDE:**
## It watches your listings for you
Every morning it checks and only speaks when something's actually due:
- Photos landed → offers your tour
- Open house in five days → builds the kit
- A week since your last seller update → writes it
- Went under contract → writes your sold content early
**Most mornings it says nothing. That's on purpose.**

**SAY:** This turns on by itself. It's not a daily nag — if nothing's due, you hear nothing. Say "stop
watching my listings" any time and it stops for good.

## SLIDE 27 — The rules it follows

**ON SLIDE:**
## The rules it follows
- **Never invents anything about a home** — a missing fact gets flagged, never guessed
- **Fair housing checked** on everything public — it sells the house, never the neighbours
- **Never prints a placeholder disclaimer** — if yours isn't set, it leaves a blank and tells you
- **Never predicts a price** or promises a result
- **Never posts, sends, or mails** without you saying yes
- **Never tells you which offer to take**

**SAY:** Everything public gets a compliance check. It will leave a gap and tell you rather than make
something up about a house it's never seen. That rule protects you.
